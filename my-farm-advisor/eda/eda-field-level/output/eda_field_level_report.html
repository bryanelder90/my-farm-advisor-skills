2. Field Boundary Geometry

Analysis method: Field boundaries loaded as GeoJSON via GeoPandas, projected to EPSG:5070 (Conus Albers Equal Area) for accurate area and perimeter computation. Compactness computed as the isoperimetric quotient 4πA / P², where 1.0 = perfect circle, ~0.79 = square, lower values = more irregular.

Area histogram (statistical): Illinois fields are smaller (mean 104 ac, range 3–305 ac) and more variable. Iowa fields are the largest (mean 184 ac, up to 634 ac). Nebraska has the smallest average size (81 ac) but includes both tiny (16 ac) and large (232 ac) fields.
Compactness boxplot (comparison): Illinois has the most irregular shapes (median 0.52, with values as low as 0.06). Iowa fields are the most regular/square (median 0.63). Nebraska shows a wide spread — some near-perfect circles (compactness up to 1.0, center-pivot irrigation) alongside irregular fields. Reference lines at 0.79 (square) and 0.60 (irregular threshold) aid interpretation.
Area vs perimeter scatter (correlation): Three distinct clusters emerge — Illinois fields scatter below the square line (irregular), Iowa fields cluster near the square line, and Nebraska fields straddle between square and circle lines. The theoretical circle and square curves show the geometric bounds.

5. Weather

Analysis method: Daily weather data from NASA POWER (2021–2025) loaded from per-field weather/daily_weather.csv files. Growing season defined as April–September. GDD computed as ((Tmax + Tmin) / 2) − 10, clipped at zero.

6. Limitations, Missing Data, and Assumptions

Data Limitations

  • Weather coverage: Per-field daily weather data is complete for all 10 Nebraska fields, but only the original 3 fields have weather data in Illinois and Iowa. The 7 expanded fields per grower were added after initial weather download. Weather visualizations for IL and IA are based on fewer data points, though the climate signal is representative of those counties.
  • CDL coverage: Complete for all 30 fields across all three growers (2021–2025).
  • Field boundary coverage: Complete for all 30 fields from OpenStreetMap.

Assumptions

  • Growing season: Defined as April–September for all growers. This is appropriate for corn and soybeans across the Corn Belt but may not capture the full season for winter wheat in Nebraska.
  • GDD base temperature: 10°C is used as the base for all growers, standard for corn.
  • CDL rotation quality: Rotation sequences derive from the dominant crop per year per field. Fields with multiple crops in a single year are represented by the most common crop class.
  • Weather spatial resolution: NASA POWER provides grid-cell averages at 0.5° × 0.625° resolution. Fields within the same grid cell share identical weather values.

Excluded Analyses

  • Soil analysis: Not required and intentionally excluded. SSURGO soil data is available in the pipeline for each field but was outside the assignment scope.
  • Satellite / NDVI: Not included. NDVI downloads were incomplete for the expanded fields and yield proxy analysis was deprioritized.
  • Yield data: No yield data exists in the pipeline; yield-based analyses were dropped.
  • Interactive dashboards: All outputs are static PNGs. No interactive components were built, per assignment requirements.
